The tasting

Seven courses.
One fire.

The menu turns with the wood pile and the market. This is tonight’s. Tomorrow’s will disagree with it.

I

Embered oyster, smoked cream

Shucked to order, kissed shut over coals, opened again at the table. Finished with cultured cream smoked over the morning’s ash.

Coal bed · 90 sec
II

Charred leek, hazelnut, brown butter

Leeks buried whole in embers until black, peeled to the sweet heart. Hazelnut praline and butter taken right to the edge.

Buried · 40 min
III

Sourdough, bone-marrow butter

Levain baked in the dying oven each afternoon. The butter is whipped with roasted marrow and a shameful amount of salt.

Oven fall · 240°
IV

Scallop, brown-butter dashi

Seared on steel laid directly in the fire. The dashi is made from the kombu we dry above the hearth all week.

Steel plate · flash
V

Duck, cherry, five-spice ash

Dry-aged fourteen days, hung over the fire, rested longer than you’d believe. Cherries roasted in cast iron with their stones in.

Hung · 14 days
VI

Burnt honey, sheep’s yogurt, thyme

Honey cooked past caution into something darker. Frozen sheep’s milk yogurt, thyme flowers, olive oil pressed this winter.

Caramel · 152°
VII

Woodsmoke canelé

The mold lives by the fire. The custard tastes faintly of the room you just spent three hours in. Take one for the walk home.

The room · twenty-two chairs
The room

Twenty-two chairs around a burning heart.

The building was a blacksmith’s shop for eighty years. We kept the brick, the beams, and the idea: everything here is made in front of you, with heat and patience.

No music over the fire. It has its own opinion of the evening.

The kitchen

Cooking is just listening to heat.

Chef-patron Marta Ilić spent nine years at fire-led kitchens on three continents before finding the old blacksmith's shop on Coal Lane. She cooks with two woods, one hearth, and no thermometers — the fire tells her when it's done, and it has not been wrong yet.

The brigade is six. Everyone butchers, everyone bakes, everyone splits wood. The menu changes when the market or the weather insists, which is often.
